# Documentation writing and review

[Documentation index](README.md)

## Reference

Use ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE), Issue 9, dated January 15, 2025.
The [official website](https://asd-ste100.org/) identifies this edition.
The [official standard](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) contains the writing rules and controlled dictionary.

Use [Project terms](terms.md) for technical meanings.
Preserve source papers, direct quotations, exact identifiers, and interface labels.
Do not change a technical requirement to obtain simpler wording.

## Author procedure

1. Identify the reader and the necessary action or information.
2. Use one project term for each concept.
3. Use each general word with its approved meaning and part of speech.
4. Define necessary technical nouns and technical verbs.
5. Use active sentences with a clear subject.
6. Keep procedure sentences within 20 words.
7. Give one instruction in each procedure sentence.
8. Put a necessary condition before its instruction.
9. Keep descriptive sentences within 25 words.
10. Keep each paragraph to one topic and a maximum of six sentences.
11. Explain abbreviations at first use.
12. Preserve exact paths, commands, units, and identifiers.

Use periods, commas, colons, and parentheses.
Do not use semicolons or em dashes in project prose.
Use command form for procedures.
These instructions summarize selected rules. They do not replace the standard.

## Review procedure

1. Compare implementation statements with the cited code.
2. Compare scope statements with the current project decisions.
3. Distinguish measured results from objectives and proposals.
4. Do a sentence and paragraph length check.
5. Examine general words in the official dictionary.
6. Examine technical terms for one consistent meaning.
7. Make sure that local links and named commands are correct.
8. Record unresolved language and technical issues.

An automated word count does not establish correct meanings or parts of speech.
A technical term must identify a specific software concept or process.
Do not classify an ordinary word as technical solely to avoid a dictionary restriction.
